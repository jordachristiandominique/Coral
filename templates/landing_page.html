Our Approach

CoralSense brings survey records, image analysis, and location mapping into one workspace. Researchers upload underwater images with survey details, review detected Hard Coral and Soft Coral boxes, and save coverage results for comparison and Excel export.

Results support coral cover monitoring. They do not assess coral health, bleaching, or water quality.

How It Works

From field photographs to reviewed survey results, CoralSense helps researchers organize each stage of coral cover analysis.

Field Survey

Researchers capture underwater photos during scheduled reef monitoring dives.

Step 01

Upload & Organize

Create a survey repository with its area, date, surveyors, and coordinates, then upload images by transect.

Step 02

AI-Assisted Detection

The model detects Hard Coral, Soft Coral, and the quadrat area. Coral boxes within the quadrat support estimated areas and ten representative markers.

Step 03

Review & Save

Check and edit the quadrat, coral boxes, and markers. HCC is the percentage of markers assigned to Hard Coral; saved results use Categories A–D.

Step 04

Dashboard Insights

Explore survey summaries, benthic substrate composition, and mapped HCC results. Download Excel files for further analysis.

Hard Coral Cover Guide

HCC Assessment Scale

Licuanan (2020) scale, benchmarked to the Philippine national average (22.8%) and the Tubbataha Reefs benchmark.

HCC counts Hard Coral markers only. Soft Coral and Other Substrate are excluded from the numerator. Site HCC is the mean of the transect means.

Category A

HCC: more than 44%

Reef coral cover is more than double the Philippine national average — comparable to or exceeding Tubbataha Reefs, one of the country's healthiest reef systems.

Category B

HCC: more than 33% up to 44%

Reef coral cover exceeds both the national average and the Tubbataha benchmark.

Category C

HCC: more than 22% up to 33%

Reef coral cover is above the national average, though below the Tubbataha benchmark.

Category D

HCC: 0-22%

Reef coral cover is at or below the national average. This reef may still provide meaningful ecosystem services and habitat.
